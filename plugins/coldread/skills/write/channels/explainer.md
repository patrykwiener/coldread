# Explainers: when the Must is to understand

Read this when the reader must understand how something works, such as a flow, a system or the background of a decision, rather than do or decide something.

A reader can parse a text full of defined terms and still not see how the parts fit together, so give them a picture, not only definitions.

1. Open with what the thing does, in at most five sentences.
2. Define each term the rest of the text relies on in one sentence, with one concrete example and where it lives, such as a table, a screen or a file, or say that it is new. Leave out terms the walk-through does not use.
3. Follow one case from start to end, step by step, with real values, or invented ones where the real data is confidential, before and after the change if there is one.
4. Keep what changes in the code, what someone must decide and what nobody knows yet in separate sections.

The budget covers the opening; the terms and the walk-through are the body.

When the reader is the user, the user is the real test: show the terms and the first steps of the walk-through, ask what is unclear, and only then write the rest.
